Here · after joining the Apple Developer Program

Getting Here onto phones

Seven steps. Most of them are filling in forms on Apple’s website; two are commands you paste into a black window. Set aside an afternoon for the first run, and about ten minutes for every build after that.

You do not need to download anything, and you do not need a Mac.

Apps for iPhone normally have to be built on a Mac. This one is built by a service called EAS, on Apple computers in a data centre, and it handles the security certificates on its own. Your laptop only sends the instruction.

  1. 1
    Done

    Claim the name

    Go to appstoreconnect.apple.com, choose Apps, then the + button, then New App.

    BoxWhat to put
    PlatformiOS
    NameHere: for school staff — 22 of the 30 characters Apple allows. This is the name on the listing. The name under the icon on someone’s phone stays Here, and does not have to be unique.
    Primary languageEnglish (U.S.)
    Bundle IDcom.tendedcollective.here
    SKUAnything, and nobody sees it. here-ios-1 is fine.
    User accessFull Access

    Subtitle is a separate 30-character box on the same page. Apple’s search reads the name and the subtitle together, and this name already carries the words someone would search for — school, staff — so the subtitle is free to be the warm one: A moment for yourself.

    If com.tendedcollective.here isn’t in the Bundle ID dropdown, it needs registering first: go to developer.apple.com → Identifiers, press +, choose App IDs, then App, and paste it in. Leave every tickbox on that page alone — Here doesn’t use any of them.

  3. 3
    Apple’s website5 min

    Make a key, and save the file

    This is the password that lets the build service hand your app to Apple without you being there. In App Store Connect: Users and Access → Integrations → Team Keys → +. Call it eas-submit and give it the App Manager role.

    Download the file the moment it appears. Apple shows it exactly once and will not show it again — if you close the tab, you delete that key and make another one. It ends in .p8. Keep it somewhere private, like a password manager. Note down the Key ID and Issuer ID from the same page. Don’t email it or put it in the project folder.
  4. 4
    Terminal5 min

    Connect the project to the build service

    Open Terminal (search for it in Spotlight on a Mac, or use PowerShell on Windows). Type these one at a time, pressing enter after each and waiting for it to finish:

    cd tended-app npx eas login npx eas init

    The first line moves into the project folder. The second asks you to make a free Expo account — that’s the build service, and it is separate from your Apple account. The third links this project to it.

    If Terminal says it doesn’t know what npx is, install Node from nodejs.org — take the big green button — and try again. It is the only thing you install on your own machine.

  5. 5
    Terminal25 min, mostly waiting

    Build it

    npx eas build --platform ios --profile production

    It will ask whether to create a Distribution Certificate and a Provisioning Profile. Say yes to both. These are Apple’s way of proving the app came from you; the service makes them, keeps them, and reuses them, and you never have to think about them again.

    Then it queues. Fifteen to twenty-five minutes is normal. You can close the window — it gives you a link that shows progress.

  6. 6
    Terminal10 min, then Apple waits

    Send it to Apple

    npx eas submit --platform ios --latest

    This asks for the .p8 file from step 3 and the two IDs that came with it. Then it uploads.

    Afterwards the build sits in Processing in App Store Connect for anywhere between five and thirty minutes before TestFlight will show it. Nothing is wrong. That is Apple scanning it, and there is nothing to do but wait.

  7. 7
    Apple’s website20 min

    Invite your teachers

    TestFlight is Apple’s app for trying apps before they’re public. Your testers install TestFlight from the App Store, and Here appears inside it.

    There are two ways to invite people, and the difference decides how much work is left.

Recommended for the pilot

Add them one by one

  • Up to 100 people
  • No review by Apple — live within minutes
  • Each teacher gets added to your Apple account as a user, under Users and Access. Give them the Customer Support role, which is the smallest one that still lets them test.
  • Twenty invitations to send by hand, and no waiting
Later

Share a public link

  • Up to 10,000 people
  • Apple reviews it first — usually a day or two
  • Needs a privacy policy page on your website, which doesn’t exist yet
  • Worth it once the pilot outgrows people you can add by hand

Either way, fill in the Test Information box in TestFlight and tell testers what this build is: the feed is made-up sample posts, nothing they write reaches anyone else, and nothing leaves their phone. A tester who assumes the feed is live and finds out later stops trusting everything else you tell them.

Two things to sort before you invite anyone

  • hello@tendedcollective.com has to reach a real person. It’s printed inside the app in two places, so the first tester with a problem will use it.
  • The app currently invents a fake history. On first open it fills in a week of check-ins and six weeks of habit ticks so the screens don’t look empty. It’s a switch I can turn off in a minute. Fine to leave on if you tell testers; confusing if you don’t.

Neither stops you building. Say the word on the second one and I’ll turn it off.

The developer version of this, with the exact field names and file paths, is in the project at docs/testflight.md.
